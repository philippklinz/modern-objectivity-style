Emotional Intent

Emotion can be an intentionally designed function rather than a decorative afterthought.

Intention and outcome

Emotional Intent is the experience the designer hopes to support. Subjective Experience is what a person actually feels or reports within their context. A design cannot guarantee a particular emotional reaction.

Planning an experience

  1. State the task and environment.
  2. Name the desired experience: e.g., calm, curiosity, fascination, or appropriate concern.
  3. Identify the possible design mechanisms.
  4. Consider unintended effects on different people.
  5. Preserve usable alternatives if the emotional intent is not achieved.
  6. Evaluate both behavior and subjective reports.

Illustrative scenarios

Research console: stable navigation and precise feedback can support calm and control; an overly quiet warning can conceal genuine risk.

Learning experiment: responsive simulations can foster curiosity; enjoyment alone does not establish understanding.

Spatial exhibition: motion and layered imagery can support wonder; provide reduced-motion and non-spatial access.

Over time

An experience may move from curiosity during exploration, to focus during a task, to concern at a genuine warning, to satisfaction after a confirmed result. This is a testable design hypothesis, not a mandatory script.

Evidence and ethics

Distinguish system properties, observed behavior, and subjective reports. Avoid fabricated urgency, false reassurance, covert manipulation, and user-blaming interpretations of different emotional responses.
