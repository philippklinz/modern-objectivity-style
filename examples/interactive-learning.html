Example — Interactive Learning Laboratory

Illustrative design scenario; not a report of empirical findings.

A browser laboratory lets visitors change parameters and observe the effects on a scientific model.

Functional intent: support cause-and-effect understanding, comparison, and accurate interpretation of model assumptions.

Emotional Intent: encourage curiosity and exploration without requiring excitement to complete the learning task.

Stage: understandable controls, reversible experiments, explicit source information, and accessible navigation.

Set: an expressive dynamic model or visualization that responds to input.

Risk: movement may obscure precise values or trigger discomfort. Visual fascination is not evidence of learning. A simplified simulation must not be mistaken for a full scientific measurement.

Evaluation: ask learners to describe what changed, distinguish input from output, compare trials, and identify assumptions. Separately observe exploratory actions and collect subjective reports.
