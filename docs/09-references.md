# 09 — References & Context

References provide comparisons and historical orientation. They do not serve as authority for a design rule.

## Historical context

Bauhaus, Neue Sachlichkeit, constructivist approaches, and international typographic traditions explored form, function, technology, and communication. Their appearance is not a required visual specification for Modern Objectivity.

## Contemporary systems

Study existing systems fairly:

- [Material Design](https://m3.material.io/)
- [IBM Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/)
- [Microsoft Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/)
- [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)

These already address complex interactions, accessibility, components, and motion. Modern Objectivity does not claim uniqueness for those features.

## Responsible comparisons

Define comparison criteria such as modular integration, temporal data, consistency, theme flexibility, validation, accessibility, and component governance. Avoid unsupported superiority claims.

Attribute research and respect rights in borrowed text, figures, diagrams, and code.
