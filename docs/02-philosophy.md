# 02 — Philosophy & Model

## Objectivity as a working method

Modern Objectivity is not an instruction to make interfaces visually cold or minimalist. It is a method: observe, question, reason, prototype, test, explain, improve. Design contains subjective and aesthetic choices; the method requires those choices to be distinguished from factual or measured claims.

## The formal vocabulary

- **Point:** an identifiable element, measurement, entity, position, event, or observation.
- **Line:** a relation, direction, boundary, link, development, or connection.
- **Space:** the organization of elements and their relationships, whether flat, layered, or spatial.
- **Time:** durations, transitions, system states, history, update frequency, and information freshness.

These are conceptual elements, not mandatory geometric graphics. Three-dimensional displays are not inherently superior to tables or two-dimensional diagrams.

## The Stage Model

A **stage** is the consistent designed environment in which interaction takes place. It possesses its own identity and may be restrained or expressive. A **set** is the content-specific arrangement, such as a data view, experiment, scientific visualization, or artwork. A **performance** is the actual behavior of stage and content over time.

A shared language can lead to very different stages. However, equivalent interactions within one application should remain consistent.

## Emotion as Function

Human experience is not outside the design problem. **Emotional Intent** means a deliberately planned experience, such as calm during administrative work, curiosity during learning, or fascination during exploration. **Subjective Experience** is the actual experience of a person in context. The first influences design; the second can be observed and reported, then used to revise the design.

Emotional goals are therefore both design inputs and potential outcomes. They are not guaranteed, and human experience is not simply an additional geometric primitive.

## Ethical responsibility

Do not suggest technical reliability that is absent, portray uncertainty as certainty, or use misleading urgency and hidden consequences. Emotional effectiveness must not depend on manipulation.

## Historical context

Historical movements may inspire comparative questions about technology, form, and function, but do not provide the authority or required appearance of this language.
